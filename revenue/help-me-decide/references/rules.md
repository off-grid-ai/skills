# The 22 rules

Every decision report passes all 22. The rules reviewer grades each one PASS / PARTIAL / FAIL with the place on the page.

Evidence tags: **tested** = controlled experiments; **studied** = studies of real decisions (a link, not proof of cause); **practice** = expert practice from the books.

## The first screen

1. **Open with the decision, the recommendation and the deadline.** If there is no deadline, say so. Put the question with the answer, so the answer has meaning. *practice. Minto ch. 1; Zelazny, Say It With Presentations; US Army AR 25-50 (bottom line up front); Garner's ABA column; Board Intelligence decision-paper guide.*
2. **One decision per report**, or the asks ranked. People asked to do three things did fewer of any one. *tested. Rogers & Lasky-Fink, Writing for Busy Readers.*
3. **Say the real problem and what waiting costs**, in one line, inside the strip or the first reason. *practice. Rumelt (diagnosis first); Williams (condition, cost, resolution); Smart Brevity ("why it matters").*
4. **At most seven lines on the first screen** (title + strip rows + reasons), readable without opening anything. *practice. Few (one screen); Amazon (the memo stands on its own); Board Intelligence.*
5. **A warning that could change the decision goes on the first screen**, never one level down. *practice. Tufte on the Columbia shuttle slide.*

## Each line

6. **Every line is a full statement** with a verb, and a number where possible. Headings too. Never a topic label like "Background" or "Market overview". *practice. Minto ch. 7 (no empty statements); Zelazny; Tufte; Williams.*
7. **Three to five lines per level, no overlap, nothing missing, most important first.** Every line under a parent answers the same question about it (why, or how). *practice. Minto ch. 1, 6, 7; Smart Brevity.*
8. **Plain words.** Method and book names live in the sources table only. See `plain-words.md`. *practice. US plain-language guidelines; Rogers & Lasky-Fink.*
9. **Cut whatever doesn't change the decision, then cut again.** Writers add words when asked to improve; cut on purpose. *tested. Mayer, coherence principle; Rogers & Lasky-Fink.*
10. **Bold only the recommendation.** Emphasis spread around stops working. *tested. Rogers & Lasky-Fink.*

## Clicking to open

11. **Each line sums up what is under it; opening it answers the question it raises.** One click per level. *tested. Minto (vertical question and answer); Mayer (segmenting); Few (summary first, detail behind a link); Smart Brevity ("go deeper").*
12. **Under a line, short sentences, not nested bullets.** Bullets hide "because" and "unless". *practice. Amazon memos; Tufte; Duarte, Slidedocs.*
13. **Every number sits next to its label and something to compare it with** (target, last period, a similar case). If there is nothing to compare with, say so. *tested. Mayer (spatial contiguity); Tufte ("compared to what?"); Few.*

## Sources

14. **Every claim links to a source row**: where in the source, what it supports, type (own text / summary / second-hand), and the date read. Reviews by agents are listed as sources too. *practice. Tufte, Beautiful Evidence; US intelligence standard ICD 203, standard 1; tyroneross strict trace.*
15. **Every line is labelled fact, estimate or judgment.** No other labels except "against" for the case against. *practice. Kahneman, Lovallo & Sibony, question 7; ICD 203, standard 3.*
16. **Odds are numbers**, a range, compared with how similar cases went. If nothing similar exists, say so and mark confidence low. Odds are not the same as source confidence; see `precedence.md`. *tested. Tetlock, Superforecasting; ICD 203, standard 2.*
17. **Show the case against the recommendation**, labelled, inside the reason it attacks. *studied. Lovallo & Sibony, McKinsey Quarterly 2010; ICD 203, standard 6.*

## The decision

18. **At least two real choices plus "do nothing".** Never a bare yes or no. *studied. Nutt via Heath, Decisive (52% vs 32% failure; second-hand); Kahneman, Lovallo & Sibony, question 5.*
19. **The decider sets the points before scoring; a separate reviewer scores; the page shows what would change the answer.** Points that changed between drafts are shown with both versions. *studied. Noise (mediating assessments); Kepner-Tregoe; Conn & McLean, Bulletproof Problem Solving ch. 2.*
20. **List ways it could fail, each with a fix, and set a check-in date.** Imagine it has already failed and ask why. *tested (modest). Klein's pre-mortem, after Mitchell, Russo & Pennington 1989; Heath (tripwires).*
21. **Name the one person who decides, and who does the next step.** *practice. Bain, "Who Has the D?"; Zelazny.*
22. **A separate reviewer checks the report against these rules.** The writer never checks or scores its own work. *practice. Kahneman, Lovallo & Sibony; Sibony; tyroneross strict trace.*
