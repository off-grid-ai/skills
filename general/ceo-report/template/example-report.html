Six ways this could fail, each with a fix

›

Imagine it is May 2027 and the skill failed. Why?

  • Reports grew too long to read. Fix: seven-line first screen, checked by the reviewer.
  • The writer checked its own work. Fix: a separate reviewer on a separate model.
  • Sources didn’t support the claims. Fix: the copied checker traces every number to a place in its source.
  • The build took far longer than planned. Fix: build the first screen and source checker first, then the scoring.
  • Rules drifted as we edited them. Fix: rerun the copied test cases, plus our own, after every change.
  • Books disagreed and the skill picked one at random. Fix: a written order of which book wins.

The skill will follow these 22 rules

›

tested proven in experiments. studied found in studies of real decisions. practice expert practice. The grey line names the sources.

The first screen

  1. Open with the decision, the recommendation and the deadline. practice
    Minto · Zelazny · US Army · Garner · Board Intelligence
  2. One decision per report, or the asks ranked. tested
    Rogers & Lasky-Fink
  3. Say the real problem and what waiting costs. practice
    Rumelt · Williams · Smart Brevity
  4. At most seven lines, readable without opening anything. practice
    Few · Amazon · Board Intelligence
  5. A warning that could change the decision goes on the first screen. practice
    Tufte

Each line

  1. A full statement with a verb, and a number where possible. Headings too. practice
    Minto · Zelazny · Tufte · Williams
  2. Three to five lines per level, no overlap, most important first. practice
    Minto · Smart Brevity
  3. Plain words. Method names stay in the sources, never in the text. practice
    US plain-language guidelines
  4. Cut whatever doesn’t change the decision, then cut again. tested
    Mayer · Rogers & Lasky-Fink
  5. Bold only the recommendation. tested
    Rogers & Lasky-Fink

Clicking to open

  1. Each line sums up what is under it; opening it answers the question it raises. tested
    Minto · Mayer · Few
  2. Under a line, short sentences, not nested bullets. practice
    Amazon · Tufte · Duarte
  3. Every number sits next to its label and something to compare it with. tested
    Mayer · Tufte · Few

Sources

  1. Every claim links to a source row: where in the source, what it supports, date read. practice
    Tufte · US intelligence standard
  2. Every line is labelled fact, estimate or judgment. practice
    Kahneman, Lovallo & Sibony · US intelligence standard
  3. Odds are numbers, compared with similar cases where they exist. tested
    Tetlock
  4. Show the case against the recommendation. studied
    Lovallo & Sibony

The decision

  1. At least two real choices plus “do nothing”. studied
    Nutt · Kahneman, Lovallo & Sibony
  2. Points are set before scoring, and a separate reviewer scores. Show what would change the answer. studied
    Noise · Kepner-Tregoe · Bulletproof Problem Solving
  3. List ways it could fail, each with a fix, and set a check-in date. tested
    Klein · Heath
  4. Name who decides and who does the next step. practice
    Bain · Zelazny
  5. A separate reviewer checks the report against these rules. practice
    Kahneman, Lovallo & Sibony · Sibony
