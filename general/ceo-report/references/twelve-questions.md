# Twelve questions to test the reasoning

From Kahneman, Lovallo & Sibony, "Before You Make That Big Decision", HBR June 2011, in plain words. The rules reviewer answers all twelve on its own; where it disagrees with the writer, its answer goes on the page. Never skip a question.

| # | Question | Flag when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does the writer gain from this? | The writer or recommender benefits from the pick. |
| 2 | Is the writer too attached to the plan? | Earlier drafts reached the same answer, or the writer also does the work. |
| 3 | Did anyone disagree, and was that explored? | Nobody argued for another option. |
| 4 | Does it lean on one famous success? | A single example or comparison carries the case. |
| 5 | Are real alternatives shown? | Fewer than two real options plus "do nothing" were scored. |
| 6 | What will we wish we knew in a year? Can we get it now? | Obvious missing data could be fetched now. |
| 7 | Where did the numbers come from? | Any number lacks a source row or a fact/estimate label. |
| 8 | Does it assume success elsewhere carries over? | It relies on "it worked there, so it will work here". |
| 9 | Is anyone defending a past choice? | Points or facts changed between drafts without being shown, or money already spent drives the pick. |
| 10 | Is the best guess too hopeful? | The odds have no comparison with similar cases. |
| 11 | Is the worst case bad enough? | Failure modes all have neat fixes; slow build, drift and cost overrun are missing. |
| 12 | Is the plan too cautious? | The safe option wins only because nobody priced the upside. |

Show the table on the page as "Twelve questions test the reasoning: N pass, M flagged".
