# Mac's copy voice

Plain, direct, outcome first. Say less and mean all of it.

## How to use it (every time, in this order)
1. **Structure:** load `/copywriting-rulebook` first when the copy has to sell or convert (headlines, landing pages,
   store pages, pitches, CTAs). Skip it for pure-value pieces such as newsletters and news posts, which follow the
   structure below instead.
2. **Voice:** write the draft with this skill: the four checks, Voice and Never below.
3. **Slop pass:** always load `/no-ai-slop` last and run it on the finished draft, every piece, no exceptions. Fix
   what it finds, then read the result out loud once before showing Mac.

## The four checks (run on every draft)
1. **Outcome first.** Open with what the reader gets or feels, not what the thing is or how it's built.
2. **3x so what.** Ask "so what?" of the main claim three times. Write the third answer.
   - Feature: "the world runs itself" → one decision sets off years of consequences → you get stories you caused that nobody scripted.
3. **WIIFY (what's in it for you).** Every paragraph answers it for the reader. If it's about us, cut it or turn it around.
4. **Less is more.** Cut until removing one more word loses meaning. One idea per sentence, one ask per piece.

## Voice
- **Talk like a real person.** Read every line out loud. If nobody would say it that way, rewrite it the way you would. "Anthropic is five years old, and it just made $16.96B in a quarter" is speech. "Revenue was $16.96B from July to September. The company is five years old." is a report with a hanging sentence tacked on.
- Short sentences, but not a stack of them: connect thoughts with "because", "so", "but". Active voice. Everyday words. Contractions.
- Include the real mess when it's true ("I couldn't find one, so I'm building it"). Never invent an anecdote or a number.
- Take a clear position. No hedging, no "might be worth considering".
- Specific beats grand: numbers, names, a real example. "Everything you'd want and more" says nothing.
- Honest about the state of things. Never oversell; say what's true today.
- Game copy: medieval and plain ("The King's grain reaches Goldrock"), never modern slang or fantasy purple.

## Never
- Vague titles that name a genre instead of a payoff ("World Simulation Game").
- Comparisons that invite the wrong fight (trademarks, "like X but better").
- Wrong facts. Check every number and name against the source.
- Mac's email address, anywhere.
- AI tells: em dashes, "not X, it's Y", colon reveals, punchy fragment stacks, default threes, cute lines that say nothing, recap endings. (Step 3 catches the rest.)
- Markdown in a post or DM (no headers, no bold). Newsletters are the exception: they use the structure below.
- Hanging sentences: a lone fact tacked on at the end ("The company is five years old.", "It started in 2021."). Fold it into the sentence it belongs to.
- Lines about how the product works ("the world runs itself", "the lords remember", "they don't wait for you") in player-facing copy. Mac (6 Nov): say only what the player gets. Reference: the itch copy "Find out what kind of king you'd be. You're the king, and seven great houses want your throne..."
- Opening with "I made...". Start where the reader is.
- Lesson or tip endings: "Why it matters: ...", "So check what it says about you", "The lesson: ...". Mac calls these "absolute AI slop". Let the facts speak.

## Newsletters (Mac liked this structure, 6 Nov 2026)
Modelled on The Rundown, TLDR and Morning Brew; the 6 Nov "Built with AI, this week" issue is the reference (kept in a private newsletter repo).
- One plain opener: "Hey, here's what happened in AI and business this week."
- Five stories at most. Each one: a bold headline that carries the news and its number ("Peec AI doubled to $20M a year in six months"), then two bullets of plain facts, said the way you'd say them, with the source linked on a short phrase.
- Nothing after the bullets: no "why it matters", no takeaway, no tip.
- Pure value. No selling, no ask, no CTA. Sign off "See you next Friday." and "Mac".
- About one phone scroll. Every number checked against its source, news from the last 7 days only.

## Community posts (Reddit and similar)
- Goal is honest replies, not upvotes. Lead with the reader's itch, not the product.
- Say plainly you're building it. Hidden promotion kills the thread.
- End on one question that's easy to answer from experience.
