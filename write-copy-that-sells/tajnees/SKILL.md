# Tajnees lines

Given a concept, write 2-4 original lines that sound like a friend talking, but are tightly built underneath.

## The devices

1. **Tajnees (paronomasia).** Two words that sound almost the same and mean different, ideally opposing, things. Put them close together so the sound arrives first and the meaning lands a beat later. This is the core; every piece needs at least one pair.
   A sharper form is **tajnees-e-taam**: the *same* word used in two meanings (kal = yesterday and tomorrow).
2. **Talmeeh (allusion). Optional, use sparingly.** Drop a name only when that name belongs to *this* concept. Ghalib and Mir are not a default; most pieces need no name at all.
3. **Inkisaar (humility). Optional.** Use it only when the speaker would really say it.
4. **Plain words and concrete things.** Everyday Hindi-Urdu, the way people talk. The images come from the concept's own world (a tiffin, a ration line, a school fee receipt, a cracked wall), not from the ghazal stockroom.

## Don't imitate the surface

The aim is the craft, not Anuv Jain's vocabulary. So:
- Don't open with "Na ___ hoon, na ___" or any list of who the speaker isn't. That's one song's move, not the style.
- Don't reach for shayar, Ghalib, Mir, Faiz, haveli or mehfil unless the concept is literally about them.
- Vary the shape: a question, a plain statement, a contrast across two lines, or a short final line that undercuts the rest.
- Across the options, use different pairs *and* different structures.

## Pair bank (extend freely)

- safar / sifar (journey / zero)
- dawa / dua (medicine / prayer)
- wafa / jafa (faithfulness / cruelty)
- ishq / ashk (love / tear)
- sabr / qabr (patience / grave)
- khwaab / jawaab (dream / answer)
- qaasid / haasid (messenger / the envious one)
- raasta / vaasta (path / connection)
- mehfil / mushkil (gathering / difficulty)
- yaad / aabaad (memory / inhabited)
- qarz / farz (debt / duty)
- chhaala / ujaala (blister / light)
- thakaan / makaan (fatigue / house)
- kal / kal (yesterday / tomorrow)

The pair bank is a starting point. Coin new pairs from the concept's own vocabulary first.

Good new pairs differ by one or two sounds and pull in opposite directions. A plain rhyme with no tension in meaning doesn't count.

## How to write

1. Find the feeling inside the concept: what is lost, wanted, or ironic about it.
2. Pick a pair whose two halves are the two sides of that feeling.
3. Add a name only if it sharpens the line, and add the humility turn if the speaker would say it.
4. Keep the end rhyme loose and natural. Read it aloud; if it sounds recited rather than spoken, simplify.

## Output

- Give two or three options, each 2-4 lines, in Roman Hindi-Urdu.
- Under each, one short line in English naming the pair and what it does. Nothing more.
- Write only original lines. Never quote or lightly rework Anuv Jain's or any other songwriter's lyrics.

## Example

Concept: leaving your city for a job.

> Station pe maa ne tiffin diya, aur kaha "jaldi aana",
> safar samjha tha jisko, woh sifar nikla saara.

*safar / sifar: the journey turns out to be a zero.*
